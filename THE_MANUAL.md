## Gemini Search overview on query: "lego manual style guide"

LEGO manual design relies on a structured visual language, standard iconography, and specialized digital design tools.

Visual & Brand Identity Standards

• Typography: LEGO uses the custom LEGO Chalet typeface family (weights 60, 70, 80, Comprime, and Italics) for design and headlines, with Verdana or Arial as standard fallback fonts. [1] (<https://www.scribd.com/document/481118442/LEGO-BrandManual-1HY16-v2-pdf>)
• Color System: Corporate branding builds around core palette values: LEGO Red (Pantone 485 C), Yellow (109 C), Blue (300 C), and Green (347 C). [1] (<https://www.scribd.com/document/481118442/LEGO-BrandManual-1HY16-v2-pdf>)
• Modern Cover Redesign: LEGO has shifted physical and digital manuals toward minimalist white or tan background renders with clear element outlines to align with sustainable paper packaging initiatives. [1] (<https://ramblingbrick.com/2022/05/28/the-new-building-instruction-design-official-statement-and-editorial-rant/>)

Common Instruction Symbols

LEGO manuals use a universal, wordless pictorial system to guide builders:
• + Plus Symbol: Indicates pieces not found in the current numbered bag (sourced from loose/unnumbered bags, separate boxes, or future steps).
• Rotate / Flip Arrow: Tells the builder to turn or flip the physical model to attach the next component.
• 1:1 Scale Ratio Bar: A life-size measurement guide used to verify correct part lengths (such as Technic axles).
• Axle Length Numbers: A digit inside a circle next to an axle denoting its exact stud length (e.g., a "3" means three studs long).
• Sticker Icon: A small page/sticker graphic paired with a number mapping to the sticker sheet.
• Multi-Builder Symbol: Figures showing how many people can assemble that section together.
• Warning Symbols: Projectile caution symbols (avoid aiming at eyes) and light-brick battery tag reminders. [1] (<https://bricksfanz.com/a-guide-to-lego-instructions-symbols/>), [2] (<https://www.instagram.com/reel/DOZPdj7kvCR/?hl=en>), [3] (<https://www.instagram.com/reel/CqY64tWp2FV/?hl=en>), [4] (<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/J6221zcdHw8>)

How to Create LEGO-Style Manuals Yourself

Community builders and designers typically generate official-style step-by-step guides using specialized software:
• BrickLink Studio (Studio Instruction Maker): The most popular modern tool. It allows you to build models from the bottom up, break builds into submodels, convert submodels into callout boxes, customize step outlines (e.g., yellow highlights around new parts), and export directly to PDF or PNG. [1] (<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tX4M5xbzV24&t=389>), [2] (<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9tlXbaN4b1c>)
• LDraw & LPub: Classic open-source tools where LDraw handles the 3D model files and LPUB (such as LPub 4) generates step-by-step page layouts, as discussed by creators on Stack Exchange. [1] (<https://bricks.stackexchange.com/questions/380/how-do-i-create-manuals-in-the-same-style-as-lego-instruction-manuals>)
You can access official digital guides or download missing booklets through the LEGO Building Instructions Portal or the interactive LEGO Builder App. [1] (<https://www.lego.com/en-us/service/building-instructions>)
